## Medical Insurance Underwriting

1. **KYC & Compliance Verification**
   Before risk assessment begins, verify the applicant's identity and eligibility: 
   - Passport document
   - proof of address
   - sanctions/PEP (politically exposed person) screening 
  This is a regulatory/AML requirement, not a risk-pricing step, but incomplete or failed KYC blocks underwriting from proceeding and can trigger an automatic decline regardless of medical risk.

2. **Application & Medical Declaration**
   Applicant completes a health questionnaire disclosing pre-existing conditions, current medications, past surgeries/hospitalizations, and family medical history.

3. **Age & Demographic Risk Screening**
   Age bands, gender, occupation, and lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol use, hazardous activities/sports) are assessed, since these strongly correlate with claims frequency and severity.

4. **Pre-Existing Condition Assessment**
   - Determine whether a condition is disclosed and how it affects eligibility.
   - Apply **exclusions** (condition permanently excluded from cover), **waiting periods** (condition covered only after a set time on the policy), or **loadings** (extra premium for elevated risk).
   - For travel insurance specifically, pre-existing conditions are a major driver of claim denials, so accurate disclosure and clear policy wording are critical.

5. **Medical Evidence Gathering**
   For higher sums insured, older applicants, or flagged conditions, request additional evidence: medical exams, doctor's reports (attending physician statements), lab results, or teleunderwriting interviews.

6. **Risk Classification / Rating Tiers**
   Applicants are grouped into standard, substandard (rated up), or declined categories based on the aggregated medical risk, sometimes using actuarial mortality/morbidity tables or automated underwriting rules engines.

7. **Coverage Limits & Sub-Limits**
   Set overall sum insured plus medical-specific sub-limits (e.g., emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, evacuation/repatriation, dental emergencies, maternity) appropriate to the risk profile. This is the underwriter *selecting and adjusting* cover for an individual applicant within an already-built product — distinct from **medical cover setup**, the upfront product/actuarial work of designing the plan template and rate tables that underwriters then apply.

8. **Pricing / Loading Application**
   Calculate the base premium, then apply any medical loadings, age-banding multipliers, or destination/duration factors (for travel medical cover) to reach the final premium.

9. **Decision, Terms & Policy Issuance**
   Approve as standard, approve with exclusions/loadings, or decline. Clearly document any medical exclusions or waiting periods on the policy schedule so claims can be assessed consistently later.

10. **Ongoing Claims Feedback Loop**
    Medical claims experience is fed back into underwriting guidelines and pricing models to keep risk assessment accurate over time (e.g., adjusting loadings for conditions that are producing higher-than-expected claims).

### Key Considerations Specific to Medical/Travel Medical Underwriting
- **Disclosure accuracy**: Non-disclosure or misrepresentation of medical history is the leading cause of claim disputes.
- **Age thresholds**: Many travel medical policies have hard age cutoffs or require medical screening above a certain age (e.g., 65+, 70+).
- **Destination risk**: Cost of medical treatment varies drastically by country (e.g., US healthcare costs vs. regional averages), affecting pricing and sub-limits.
- **Trip duration**: Longer trips increase exposure to both illness and pre-existing condition flare-ups.
- **Regulatory compliance**: Medical underwriting must comply with local insurance regulations on permissible risk factors and non-discrimination rules.
- **KYC vs. underwriting**: KYC confirms *who* the applicant is (compliance/AML); underwriting assesses *what risk* they present (health, age, destination). They run in parallel at intake, but a KYC failure overrides the underwriting outcome.
- **Family size**: For family/group travel medical plans, the number of dependents affects pricing (per-head vs. flat family rate, child discounts), risk aggregation (each member can trigger an independent claim), and how sub-limits are allocated (shared vs. per-person).
